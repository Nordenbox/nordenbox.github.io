Shame

A screenplay about a girl, memory, and the private violence hidden inside an ordinary home.

INT. Olena 'S HOME MORNING

Fade in, pale face and fragile 12-year-old girl, illuminated by the light from a cheap cellphone in the darkness.

She lies on her stomach under a slender duvet, and one hand waves two small figures, a little girl and a mother.

Olena is singing in a very low voice, the song 'Journey to the Past' from Anastasia （a musical about a lost Imperial Russian princess).

Olena

(intimidates little girl's singing)

Heart don't fail me now Courage don't desert me

Don't turn back now that we're here..

People always say Life is full of choices..

She manipulates the figurine of the little girl spinning and bowing.

Suddenly, a faint sound comes from a distance, like someone opens the door with a heavy keys.

Singing stops, and Olena's hand waves the figure mother as she imitates her voice in lower voice than before.

Olena'mom

Wow, nice singing. My dear. Pursue your dream, You could be a good singer!

Olena

Thank you! Mom. I must leave now..

Olena quickly hides the figures under her pillow, then rolls over and pretends to be asleep.

Camera focus on the undisclosed door, observing the sitting room as the main gate opens and an ambiguous figure steps in.

We can see a man walking toward bathroom, shedding his black down jacket to reveal an undershirt beneath and the blood-stained skin.

Upon entering the bathroom, he places a pair of boxing gloves aside and cleans the blood and abrasions on the back of his hands.

Camera doesn't capture his face, only parts of his body.

The dim morning light seeps through a cracked curtain, hinting at the sparse and modest environment around her.

Olena wakes up from a heavy jolt on her shoulder caused by a man's sudden movement.

man

(in Ukrainian)Get up! sweaty!

A close-up shot follows Olena’s face as it lifts from the pillow, revealing a confined and poor apartment behind her. The shot continues as her body leaves the bed, and Olena walks into a bathroom without a door, clutching a yellow blanket draped over her shoulder.

She hides the toy figures in closet on the bathroom's wall. As she moves some sundries aside, a worn photograph peeks through - a woman standing before the old town walls and Golden Gate, Kyiv.

In the photo, we can notice that a middle-aged man in a chef suit standing beside her.

After hiding it, she turns body and begins to brushes her teeth.

Now we switch to Olena’s POV from the bathroom's mirror: a cramped and modest Montreal cheap apartment.

In the living room, a man in his forties is meticulously ironing a formal suit. He is still wearing his undershirt, bald head, very strong.

Nearby, a newly unboxed phone rests on a small low table. The TV box plays the UEFA Champions League, commentary filling the modest space.

On the wall, we can see some photos portraying some background of the man: a group photo of him with the football players of FC Shakhtar Donetsk Youth, 1993 Ukrainian Youth League Top Scorer, standing in front of the restaurants named Mama's Kitchen(Donetsk) and Little Ukraine's Kalyna in Montreal.

This is Olena's father, Markus, the man in the previous photo.

Suddenly, a sound of glass breaking emanates from behind, Markus peers back, noticing the backyard windows shattered amid teenager's laughter.

He rushes towards the broken glass, shouting outside with Ukrainian dirty words.

Markus

I will kill you bastards!

He angrily smashes the remaining window panes with a punch.

Olena stands in the living room.

Olena

Dad, we have no money to pay the glasses!

Markus turns his body, embarrassingly smiles.

Markus

OK, now we have no reason to stay at home, doesn't it? We do the running.

Olena

I'm not comfortable today, dad.

Markus strides towards his daughter, embracing her like a bear, and whispers.

Markus

You must do what I want you to do. Do you understand? Sweat heart. Do it now.

Olena exits the bathroom and hurriedly puts on a sweatshirt. She walks to the window and gazes outside before moving to the wardrobe, where she takes out thin sportswear to wear.

She briefly glances at a thick piece of clothing beside it, hesitating momentarily. The wall clock tells us that it is 6:30 in the morning.

When Olena reaches the doorway Markus is standing there earlier with his sports suit.

Olena

When did you come back? You didn't sleep the whole night?

Markus

No, I came back earlier, as usual.

Olena

No, you came back at 5 o'clock!

Markus

You don't trust me? Do you? I do everything because I am fighting for my family!

Markus grows angry, his face flushes, manifesting a horrifying figure.

Olena

Dad, I can help you in your restaurant.

With hesitation, Markus says.

Olena

No, I have my business. You do the thing you must do.

INT./EXT. APARTMENT CORRIDOR - DAY

Following Olena’s point of view, she looks down the corridor of the apartment’s RC. It appears like a dark tunnel leading to a bright, cold world beyond.

Olena rushes out of the apartment, meeting the extreme cold of Montreal’s winter. She slips and falls.

Markus stands there, watching as Olena picks herself up from the ground, his shouts echoing in the frosty air, hands waving like a boxer's.

Markus

Stand up, you can do this!

EXT. STREET - DAY

On the street, snow falls gently; the pale air is as cold as an icy wall. In Olena's view, her father's running figure seems unreachable, always just out of reach. On both sides of the street, the 1980s tenement buildings stand old and dark, like ominous monsters hunching behind the falling snow.

The camera follows Olena as she runs, capturing the sound of her exhausted breath and the sight of her trembling body.

Olena’s speed slows gradually, but she doesn’t dare to stop. She spots her father standing ahead, gazing back at her solemnly.

She passes by Markus.

Markus

Run!

He briskly rubs his daughter's arms through the thin cloth, trying to warm her up. This behaviour emboldens Olena to speak.

Olena

Dad, I'm cold. Really cold.

Markus

No, no! You must never stop. In this world, life is difficult and cruel everywhere.

He turns back and runs. Olena continues to run. She opens her mouth, gasping for air, her breath visible in the icy air like faint smoke.

EXT. IN FRONT OF TIM'S - THE DAY

Olena sees her father stopping at Tim Horton’s gate and watching the advertisement on the glass wall.

Olena can’t stop here; she tries to go through, but her arm is caught by her father.

Markus

Wait a moment.

Olena comes to a halt, wrapping her arms tightly around her chest, trying to ward off the cold. She can't stop, hopping from foot to foot.

Markus

Keep running, go across Elm Street, Rue Clark, and Beaumont. Be back here in 20 minutes. You can do it!

He gives Olena a cellphone. It is a new smartphone, still warm from his body heat.

Markus

Take pictures at every intersection you cross with this new phone and bring them back to me. Don't break my cellphone, it's important to me!

Olena nods and continues to run.

EXT. STREET - DAY

Markus's figure slowly fades into the wintry weather, blanketing the street in white and fog. Olena turns the corner of Elm Street and Rue Clark. She stops, and her trembling body turns back, confirming that her father has disappeared from her view.

The camera moves listlessly towards Olena, capturing a tiny shift in her facial muscles. She has a glimpse of the apartment building she has been living in. It is located at the second corner of the block.

House's back window is broken, revealing a black hole to let the chilly air in.

We can see she is standing at a crossing of two streets. Olena hesitates for a moment. She changes her direction and runs.

INT. LAUNDRY ROOM - DAY

Olena steps into the cozy warmth of the public laundry room of the apartment, discovering a snug corner nestled between two washing machines. It feels like a comforting haven, a confined world of solace. She closes her eyes slowly, leaning her head against the squeaky machine.

Olena leans her body against the wall, browsing through her father's cellphone.

Flickering photos reveal her family's past: Father as a unsuccessful soccer player, retired from the team, wedding with mother, raising baby Olena, opening his own restaurant in hometown, amateur boxer, a group photo with local businessmen, 2014 Ukrainian war, mother sleeping (dying) in a hospital bed, sorrowful father...

The final photo is a Business Plan for a restaurant.

A teenage Caribbean-like boy sits nearby. He appears pale, slender, and under 16. He stares at Olena and asks (following dialogues are spoken by English):

Boy

What is your name?

Olena

Olena, I’ve never seen you before.

Boy

Nice to meet you. Olena. My name is Marcus.

Marcus steps closer and sits beside Olena, his gaze fixed on her face.

Olena

Your name is like my father's.

Marcus

What does your father do.

Olena

My father opens a restaurant and he's working hard.

Marcus

Today is not school day. Why did you get up so early?

Olena

I must run 5 km every morning because my father wants me to be a soccer player.

After a pause, Marcus says.

Marcus

Can I kiss you, a lady soccer player? You’re beautiful.

Olena smiles and says.

Olena

No, but thank you.

Olena closes the bright cellphone screen and starts to have a rest.

She notices Marcus watching her phone. He stretches his hand out toward her.

Marcus

I want this. Give me your phone! Now!

Olena grips the phone tightly, refusing to let go. Marcus struggles to take it but fails. In frustration, he picks up a metal toolbox from the ground, swings it, and strikes Olena on the head.

Olena's hand finally releases and her body lies on the ground, bleeding.

INT. TIM HORTON - DAY

Few people are inside due to the early hour.

Father Markus stands in front of the reception desk, waiting for his breakfast, speaking to the clerk. He peers back and points to the table near the window.

Markus speaks in poor English.

Markus

Make this table, for a reservation ? OK? I have an important interview today at 9 o'clock, with a man.

Clerk(OS)

OK. Bonne chance.

Markus

He is a big boss, you know, very very rich. You know.

Clerk(OS)

Good luck.

Markus

My daughter come here soon. She's a good girl. I proud of her.

His order arrives, two Farmers breakfast, sandwich combos. Markus takes the food and glimpses it. He speaks with a professional tone.

Markus

This bacon, little burnt..

The Clerk shrugs and offers wry smile.

Markus brings the food, sits down at the table, and watches the clock hanging on the wall.

A beautiful lady approaches the table, holding a plate filled with food.

LADY

Je peux m’asseoir ici?

Markus

No, I wait for my daughter.

He mutters, practicing for the upcoming interiew.

Two bagel and coffee combos are placed on the desk beside the windows, emitting vapor visible in the cold air.

arkus turns his head, gazing at the grey sky and snow falling on the ground outside the glass window, waiting his girl returning.

The song "Journey to the past" continues.

I know someone's waiting

Years of dreams just can't be wrong

Arms will open wide

I'll be safe and wanted

Finally home where I belong

Well, starting now I'm learning fast

On this journey to the past
