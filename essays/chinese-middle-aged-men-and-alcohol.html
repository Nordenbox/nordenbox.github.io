Chinese Middle-Aged Men and Alcohol: Navigating a Complex Relationship

A social scene built around food, cigarettes, and baijiu, and the emotional logic behind it.

原文链接 Open the original Medium post nordenbox.medium.com/chinese-middle-aged-men-and-alcohol-navigating-a-complex-relationship-b67bcc8072cf

The article opens at a familiar table: rich food, cigarettes, and strong liquor, with middle-aged men talking politics, business, or nothing in particular. It is a scene many readers in China will recognize immediately.

That scene is more than background decoration. It reveals a social code in which drinking is tied to hospitality, hierarchy, obligation, and the performance of adulthood. Baijiu is not just an alcohol; it is part of a shared ritual.

The writer then reflects on a personal discomfort with that culture. Over time, the disgust toward drinking changes shape. What once looked like a simple refusal becomes an attempt to understand why the ritual persists and what kind of social need it serves.

The essay finally treats alcohol as a lens on male social life. It is about camaraderie, pressure, and the emotional habits that hold gatherings together, even when no one particularly enjoys the drink itself.
