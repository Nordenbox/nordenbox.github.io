Cultural Revolution is the first love of Modernism to Chinese people and their aesthetics

A short essay on why Cultural Revolution imagery keeps reappearing in contemporary Chinese aesthetics.

原文链接 Open the original Medium post nordenbox.medium.com/cultural-revolution-is-the-first-love-of-modernism-to-chinese-people-and-their-aesthetics-a45151041fd3

The essay begins with a simple but uncomfortable question: why do Cultural Revolution images still feel visually powerful to so many Chinese viewers, especially younger ones who did not live through that period?

Part of the answer lies in the imagery itself. Posters, slogans, heroic poses, bright reds, simplified figures, and disciplined compositions still carry a strong visual charge. They do not merely inform; they stage a kind of aesthetic certainty.

The article suggests that this attraction is not only political nostalgia. It also comes from a broader encounter with modernism: the flattening of form, the force of symbols, and the thrill of rupture. In that sense, the Cultural Revolution becomes part of a longer modern visual grammar.

That helps explain why these motifs keep resurfacing in galleries, commercial design, and tourism-oriented culture. They can be quoted as history, but they can also be experienced as style.

The piece therefore treats the Cultural Revolution less as a closed historical image bank and more as an unfinished aesthetic language, one that still shapes how power, memory, and modern Chinese visuals are understood.
