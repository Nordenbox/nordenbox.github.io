Cultural Revolution is the first love of Modernism to Chinese people and their aesthetics

A short essay on why Cultural Revolution imagery keeps reappearing in contemporary Chinese aesthetics.

原文链接 Open the original Medium post nordenbox.medium.com/cultural-revolution-is-the-first-love-of-modernism-to-chinese-people-and-their-aesthetics-a45151041fd3

The essay starts from a basic puzzle: why do so many young Chinese readers and viewers find Cultural Revolution aesthetics strangely compelling?

Propaganda posters, slogan imagery, worship poses, and familiar costume cues keep resurfacing in galleries and in public visual culture, especially in places where contemporary art and mass tourism overlap.

The argument is that the attraction is not merely political nostalgia. It also comes from a deeper aesthetic memory: a taste for simplified form, high-intensity symbolism, and the visual force of modernist rupture.

In that sense, the piece treats the Cultural Revolution not as a sealed historical chapter, but as an unfinished visual grammar that still shapes how modern Chinese imagery feels.
