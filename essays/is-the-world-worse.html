Is the world worse?

A rainy conversation by a dying campfire turns into a reflection on how communication technologies shrink the world while making it louder.

原文链接 Open the original Medium post nordenbox.medium.com/is-the-world-worse-5a5c5a15c3ee

The essay begins with a question that sounds simple but is hard to answer honestly: has the world become worse? The setting is rainy, cold, and slightly exhausted, which fits the mood of the question itself.

The writer’s starting point is that modern communication has made everyone more connected while also making public life louder, faster, and more brittle. People can now speak confidently about almost anything, even when they have little direct experience of it.

From there the piece moves between private irritation and larger social diagnosis. It asks whether the feeling of decline comes from the world actually getting worse, or from the fact that we now see more of it at once.

The article also suggests that older social worlds were smaller, slower, and more local, which may have made them feel more coherent even when they were not. The contrast is not between perfect past and broken present, but between different kinds of noise.

By the end, the essay does not offer a neat verdict. Instead it leaves the reader with a sharper sense of how much of “the world is worse” is an emotional response to scale, speed, and constant exposure.
