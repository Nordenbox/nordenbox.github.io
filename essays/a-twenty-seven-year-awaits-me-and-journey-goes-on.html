A Twenty-Seven-Year Awaits Me and Journey Goes On

A long-distance love letter to Final Fantasy VII Rebirth, and to the 27 years that led back to it.

原文链接 Open the original Medium post medium.com/a-twenty-seven-year-awaits-me-and-journey-goes-on-9733e1fe02e9

The final scene of Final Fantasy VII Rebirth lands like an old wound reopening. The writer remembers sitting in a basement in Montreal at 2:30 AM, watching the last cinematic sequence play out while snow fell outside, and feeling grief, confusion, and an almost embarrassing amount of attachment all at once.

That reaction is not just nostalgia for a childhood game. It is the return of a story that first entered the writer’s life twenty-seven years earlier, in a film-school dormitory where a CRT monitor displayed blocky polygons and everyone else was already convinced that Final Fantasy VII was something important.

What stayed after that first encounter was not a single plot point, but a way of imagining a world. The series mixed steam-era machinery, melancholy skies, weirdly tender friendship, and a visual atmosphere that felt half industrial, half dreamlike. It made fantasy feel lived-in rather than decorative.

The essay treats the 27-year gap between the original encounter and Rebirth as part of the work itself. The game is not merely being replayed; it is being re-read by a person who has changed, and who now understands how memory accumulates around unfinished stories.

That is why the article keeps circling back to the hero’s journey, to the Zeigarnik effect, and to the peculiar emotional power of things that remain unresolved. A story can stay alive for decades precisely because it never fully closes.

In the end, the piece becomes a letter to a game, to a younger self, and to the long distance between first seeing a story and finally understanding why it stayed with you.
